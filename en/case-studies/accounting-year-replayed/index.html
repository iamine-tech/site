IAMINE Compta · in use

A full financial year replayed, to the cent

I wrote my company’s accounting software, then had it replay a closed financial year. It matches the accounting firm’s profit and loss account, to the cent.

By Amine Benmansour, application architect

The starting point

My company’s books are kept by an online accounting firm. I wanted to understand every entry, and to check that software could produce the same accounts from the same documents.

The scope is that of a French services company subject to corporate income tax: bank, sales, purchases, monthly VAT, the director’s social contributions, fixed assets and year-end closing.

What the software does

  • Transactions come in through the bank’s API. Each one carries an idempotency key, so a re-run import creates no duplicate.
  • Entries are double-entry. An entry moves from draft to validated, and validation is irreversible: a mistake is corrected with a reversing entry.
  • Validated entries are numbered and hash-chained, in the manner of the French statutory audit file (FEC). A change made after the fact would show.
  • Monthly VAT is computed from the general ledger. The software also keeps the social contribution schedule, fixed assets and depreciation, the balance sheet, the profit and loss account and the FEC export.

The proof: replaying a closed year

The software replayed an entire closed financial year: 925 ledger lines imported and reconciled. The resulting profit and loss account is the firm’s, to the cent: revenue, every expense block, corporate income tax and net result.

An account-by-account reconciliation classifies every gap: matching, structural, to investigate, or software bug.

Since then: a blind monthly close

Every month, the software produces its close without reading the firm’s data. The comparison only happens once the close is frozen, and every gap is classified against source documents.

What remains open: month-by-month VAT, where not every gap is explained yet.

The method

The software is written with AI coding agents, under constraints. Each slice has a written scope, explicit prohibitions and numeric expected results. It ends with a green test suite and a check against real data.

The business domain is isolated from infrastructure. Accounting rules are pure services, tested on their own, and several hundred automated tests cover the whole: unit, integration and end-to-end.

AI categorisation suggestions go through a frozen test bench, and names are masked before any call to a model.

Java 21 · Spring Boot 3 · Quarkus · PostgreSQL · Kafka · Flyway · React · TypeScript · Testcontainers · Playwright

Personal project, in use on my own company’s books, alongside the accounting firm.
